Research project brief

Research project brief
Global South Continuous Traumatic Stress Scale
GS-CTSS | Research framework and draft item pool | Hadi Omar

Research purpose
To investigate whether culturally grounded assessment can better capture the relationship between
ongoing threat, interrupted recovery, everyday functioning, and collective or structural stressors. The
project emerged from the Palestinian context and proposes subsequent adaptation with other
communities.

Current development
The foundational report contains nine proposed domains and 90 draft statements using a five-point
agreement format. Themes include daily threat, media exposure, social expectations of endurance, identity
and political pressure, economic insecurity, bodily stress, emotional detachment, community connection,
and collective survival.

Methodological priorities
Clarify the construct and intended population; distinguish exposure, distress, functioning, and protective
factors; reduce overlapping items; agree an appropriate recall period; conduct community consultation,
expert review, and cognitive interviews; then pilot under the required ethical review. Subsequent studies
would examine structure, reliability, validity, and cross-cultural comparability using a justified sampling
plan.

Interpretation and research status
The source report explicitly labels its findings as simulated. These are illustrative rather than participant-
based validation evidence. No clinical cutoff, diagnostic accuracy, treatment effect, or validated total score
is claimed here. Self-report items would not establish neurological changes or medication effects.

Collaboration sought
Paid research assistantships, supervised measurement-development projects, community partnerships,
and funded research opportunities. The project is intended to complement careful assessment of ongoing
conditions and does not establish that existing trauma diagnoses cannot apply during continuing threat.

Selected academic portfolio

Selected academic portfolio
Research methods and measurement
Coursework includes a perceived-intelligence research draft, a self-attention laboratory report, and
personality assessment development assignments. These illustrate research questions, study reporting,
and engagement with qualitative and quantitative methods. Numerical findings in coursework require data
and analysis verification before external research claims.

Trauma research
A comparative research critique and the GS-CTSS foundational report demonstrate interests in continuous
threat, adaptation, and the relationship between individual distress and social context. The scale project
remains developmental.

Cognitive behavioural approaches
A CBT review, comparative assignments, and presentation materials address formulation, behavioural
methods, exposure, mindfulness, and intervention planning. These are academic and training materials,
not evidence of independently delivered treatment outcomes.

Groups and families
Group leadership reports, a social anxiety session simulation, and family systems assignments address
structure, communication, boundaries, and facilitation. Simulation scripts demonstrate preparation for
supervised practice.

Culture and diversity
Reports and slides examine substance use, stigma, intersecting identities, access to support, and culturally
responsive communication. The portfolio supports an approach that asks about individual experience
rather than assuming beliefs from background.

Development and neuropsychology
Assignments discuss cognitive and moral development, emotion regulation, brain–behaviour relationships,
and the locus coeruleus–noradrenaline system. These demonstrate academic engagement rather than
specialist certification.

Communication and creative practice
Arabic and English reports, presentations, and reflective writing on music and site-specific art show
experience communicating across academic and creative formats. Full source works can be selected for a
particular role after individual review.
